Every organization is asking some version of the same question right now: *where do we begin with AI?*

The honest answer is that the starting point is almost never the technology itself. It's the work that happens before you pick a tool — understanding where your team loses time, where decisions are bottlenecked, and where the cost of a mistake is low enough to experiment.

## Start with friction, not features

The most useful thing you can do in the first weeks of an AI strategy engagement is inventory friction. Where do your people spend time on tasks they find tedious? Where does information get stuck moving between people or systems? Where do you repeatedly make the same kinds of decisions with imperfect information?

These are the places AI creates genuine leverage. Not because AI is magical, but because it's very good at the specific things that create friction in knowledge work: summarizing, drafting, classifying, extracting, and generating options.

## The maturity trap

One mistake I see often is organizations trying to assess their "AI maturity" before they've done anything. Maturity frameworks are useful eventually, but they can become a reason to delay action. You learn more about your actual readiness by running a focused pilot than by filling out a maturity assessment.

Pick something small, low-stakes, and high-friction. Run it for six weeks. Learn what you learn. Then decide what's next.

## What this looks like in practice

A good first engagement looks something like this: spend a few weeks talking to the people doing the work — not just leadership — and surface three to five specific friction points that are genuinely painful and genuinely tractable. Then design a pilot for one of them that can be evaluated on real outcomes within a quarter.

That's it. No grand transformation roadmap required in week one.
